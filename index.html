            What electrolytes do (and how much you need)

            Sodium is the main one you lose in sweat, roughly 460 to 1,150 mg per liter, and it varies a lot from person to person. It helps you hold on to the water you drink.

            Potassium and magnesium help muscles and nerves work. Fruit, potatoes, milk, yogurt, beans and nuts cover them for most athletes.

            When a sports drink helps: sessions over 60 to 90 minutes, hot gyms, two-a-days, or if you are a salty sweater (white streaks on your shirt). For shorter workouts, water and normal meals are enough.

            Don't stack them: one drink or mix per session is plenty. Skip electrolyte pills or high-dose magnesium and potassium supplements unless a doctor or dietitian tells you to.
